6. Prayer Lock and its limits

Prayer Lock uses Apple’s Screen Time to put a “Pray first” screen over apps you choose. It is designed to help you pause, not to make your phone unusable:

  • You choose which apps it covers, and you can switch Prayer Lock off in Altar at any time.
  • Once you have used the day’s unlocks, an emergency unlock opens your apps for 5 minutes, at most once an hour.
  • You can remove Altar’s Screen Time access in Settings › Screen Time, or delete Altar, which removes its locks.

Prayer Lock depends on iOS. Screen Time can behave differently across iOS versions, after updates, in Low Power Mode or when permissions change, and Apple controls what apps like Altar can do with it. We cannot guarantee that a lock will always appear, or always lift, exactly on time. Do not use Prayer Lock on any app you may need in an emergency, such as phone, messaging, maps, medical, banking or safety apps.

Prayer Lock is a self-control tool for your own device. Do not use it to restrict someone else’s phone without their knowledge.
